Bandcamp vs Beatport for DJs: which store for what

Bandcamp or Beatport is the wrong question for most DJs. They are good at different things, and most people who dig seriously end up using both. Here is what each is actually good for.

Beatport: the dance music default

Digital DJ Tips describes Beatport as the biggest dance music download store, with the newest releases, the widest label coverage and influential charts. The same write-up lists the drawbacks DJs complain about: higher prices, extra charges for lossless files, slow performance, weak search, VAT added at checkout and limits on re-downloads.

Filtering by key: Beatport's help center explains that you open a genre, choose the Tracks tab, then use the Key filter and tick the keys you want. It works in track lists such as My Beatport and on artist and genre pages. Third-party guides also describe BPM range filtering from the same area; menu names change, so check the current layout.

Bandcamp: artists, labels and exclusives

On Bandcamp, purchases often go directly to the artist or label. The same Digital DJ Tips article notes the interface is not designed for buying in bulk, which is why it does not replace a download store in a DJ workflow. Where Bandcamp shines is small labels, self-releases and music that never reaches the big stores. See how to search it properly.

Side by side

  • Best for new club releases and charts: Beatport.
  • Best for small labels, exclusives and supporting artists directly: Bandcamp.
  • Buying many tracks quickly: Beatport (Bandcamp is built for one release at a time).
  • Finding the unexpected: Bandcamp, if you are willing to browse by tag and region.

The real cost is searching twice

DJ TechTools' guide to digital crate digging describes a common workaround for Beatport's slow, typo-sensitive search: googling the artist name plus "Beatport" and opening the result. Doing that for several stores is where the hours go. Searching once across stores removes that step.
