A repeatable digital crate-digging workflow for DJs

Most online digging is a pile of open tabs. This workflow turns it into steps you can repeat, so an hour of digging ends in a short list of tracks you actually buy.

1. Start from seeds, not the front page

Pick three to five artists or labels whose records you already play. They are your seeds. The front pages of stores show what is promoted; seeds show what fits your sets.

2. Follow the connections

For each seed, list who remixed them, who they collaborated with and who featured on their tracks. These are the closest neighbors in a scene, and they are the names a keyword search will not suggest. Each name becomes a new seed for the next round.

3. Listen in one pass

Switching between tabs and players is where most of the time goes. Collect the candidates first, then listen to them straight through, one after another, in one player. Decide in the first half minute: keep or skip.

4. Tag as you go

One tag for the set the track belongs to, one for energy or mood, one for the key or tempo if it matters. Tags you add while listening are worth far more than notes you try to reconstruct later.

5. Keep a buy list, separate from the maybe pile

Move only the tracks you are sure about to the buy list. Review the maybe pile once a week. More on keeping the stages apart.

6. Buy in one session

Buy everything on the list in one go, store by store, then mark the tracks as owned so they never come back. This also tells you which stores actually carry your music.

Where the hours go, and how to remove them
