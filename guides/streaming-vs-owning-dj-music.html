Streaming vs owning music for DJing: what to know

Streaming inside DJ software is convenient, and for practice or casual sets it can be enough. For a library you rely on, the difference between streaming and owning matters. The facts below are from Digital DJ Tips' 2026 guide.

What the services offer

  • Spotify: over 100 million tracks inside DJ software such as Serato, Rekordbox and djay Pro, with no offline mode, so it needs internet for the whole set.
  • Apple Music: works in djay Pro, Serato, Rekordbox and Engine DJ, also with no offline capability.
  • TIDAL: its DJ Extension includes an offline mode, but the DJ features cost extra on top of the base subscription.
  • Beatport Streaming: the Advanced tier works with DJ gear; Beatport Professional adds better audio quality and an offline cache of 1,000 tracks.
  • Beatsource Streaming: the basic tier works with DJ gear; Pro+ adds an offline locker of 1,000 tracks and exclusive DJ tools.
  • SoundCloud Go+: the mainstream catalogue plus SoundCloud's producer network.

What you give up

The same guide notes "annoying technical limitations" for streaming DJs that do not affect DJs playing their own files, and that you depend on a subscription and on the service's label licences to keep access. An offline cache is not ownership: a record-pool article makes the same point about Beatport's offline "locking", saying it is not the same as owning the file.

The recent closure of Juno Download is a reminder of why this matters: a store can disappear overnight, and a library you own survives, while a library you rent only exists while the licence and subscription do.

A sensible split

  • Own the core library you play every week, and back it up.
  • Stream for discovery, practice and one-off requests, then buy what earns a place in the crate.
