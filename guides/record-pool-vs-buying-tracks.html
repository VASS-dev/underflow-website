Record pool vs buying tracks: which is better for DJs

A record pool and a download store solve different problems. One is a subscription for volume, the other is a shop for the specific record you are after.

How pools work

According to Digital DJ Tips, download pools are sold on a monthly subscription, are more cost-effective than buying tracks individually, and include versions and edits the public cannot buy. They are best for commercial music, with little or no older music. A record pool's own comparison page lists pools at roughly $10 to $35 a month; treat that as marketing and check current prices.

The main pools, as described

  • BPM Supreme: a good all-rounder with a commercial focus, many DJ re-edits and many exclusives.
  • DJcity: owned by the group behind Beatport and Beatsource; it grew out of hip-hop and covers a wide range of styles.
  • zipDJ: stronger for underground house and electronic styles.

Where a pool falls short

  • It is a flow of new releases, not a catalogue: a specific older or niche record is often not there.
  • It suits commercial and club-edit sets better than rare or underground digging.
  • The subscription is a recurring cost, whether you used it this month or not.

Where a store wins

If you know the artist or the record, a store sells you exactly that file, and bought tracks are yours to keep. See where DJs buy music for the store options.
