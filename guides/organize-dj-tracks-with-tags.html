Tags for a DJ library: a simple system that lasts

Most tag systems die because they are too big. A small set of groups you actually use beats a perfect taxonomy you abandon in a month.

1. Choose a few groups, not a taxonomy

Three or four groups cover most needs: mood, use (opener, peak, closer), key or tempo band, and genre. If a group never helps you pick a track, delete it.

2. Keep each group short

Five to eight tags per group is plenty. Underflow allows up to 7 groups and 15 tags in each group, which is a ceiling, not a target.

3. Tag while you listen

The moment you decide a track is good is the moment you know what it is for. Tag it then; reconstructing it later never works.

4. Use colors for what you scan

Give the group you use most a strong color so the list can be read at a glance in a dark booth or a late-night session.

5. Filter with AND and OR

AND narrows: warm and opener and a key you can mix into. OR widens: any of three moods. Both are available as filters in the track list, so building a set becomes selecting tags instead of scrolling.
